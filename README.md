# Nickel Plating Handbook

**A practical nickel electroplating guide maintained by
NickelPlatingPro**

This handbook teaches operators how to set up, run, control, diagnose,
and improve nickel electroplating processes without disclosing
proprietary product formulations, additive chemistry, manufacturing
methods, or trade secrets.

## Important scope

This is an **operating handbook**, not a chemical-manufacturing manual.
It intentionally does **not** publish chemical recipes, proprietary
ingredient lists, bath-making concentrations, brightener formulas,
nickel strike formulas, electroless nickel formulas, proprietary
replenishment chemistry, or NickelPlatingPro manufacturing procedures.

Operating ranges in this handbook are general industry starting points.
**Always use the specifications supplied by the manufacturer of the
commercial chemistry you are operating.**

## Core calculations

### Surface area

For a rectangular part plated on all six faces:

`Area = 2(L×W + L×H + W×H)`

Use **square feet** when current density is specified in amperes per
square foot.

### Required current

`Current (A) = Surface Area (ft²) × Current Density (A/ft²)`

**Example:** A load has 0.50 ft² of exposed area and the process
specification calls for 30 A/ft²:

`0.50 × 30 = 15 A`

The rectifier should be set to approximately **15 amperes**, subject to
the chemistry supplier's specification and actual part behavior.

### Ampere-hours

`Ampere-hours = Current (A) × Time (hours)`

A 12 A load operated for 30 minutes:

`12 × 0.5 = 6 Ah`

Track cumulative ampere-hours because additive demand and bath loading
often correlate better with electrical throughput than calendar time.
