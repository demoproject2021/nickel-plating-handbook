# 10. Bath Maintenance and Ampere-Hour Tracking

## Maintenance philosophy

Do not wait for bad parts before maintaining the bath. Preventive
control is cheaper than scrap and rework.

Track:

-   Bath volume
-   Temperature
-   pH
-   Current and plating time
-   Cumulative ampere-hours
-   Additions
-   Filtration
-   Anode condition
-   Appearance
-   Thickness
-   Hull-cell results
-   Unusual events

## Ampere-hour tracking

`Ampere-hours = Amperes × Hours`

Example: 20 A for 45 minutes:

`20 × 0.75 = 15 Ah`

Add this to the bath's cumulative total.

## Why ampere-hours matter

Electrical throughput provides a practical workload metric. Commercial
additive systems may specify replenishment or analysis based partly on
ampere-hours, but **never assume a generic replenishment rate**. Use the
product manufacturer's instructions and analytical results.

## Volume control

Evaporation changes bath volume and therefore operating condition.
Restore volume using the water quality and procedure specified by the
chemistry supplier.

## Anodes

Inspect anodes and bags for:

-   Passivation
-   Sludge
-   Poor electrical contact
-   Uneven consumption
-   Torn or clogged bags

## Never correct blindly

If appearance changes, first verify temperature, pH, current density,
contacts, agitation, filtration, preparation, and contamination history
before adding proprietary additives.
