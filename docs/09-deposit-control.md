# 9. Bright vs Satin Nickel, Plating Time, and Thickness

## Bright nickel

Bright nickel systems are designed to produce a reflective or
high-luster deposit when the substrate is properly polished and the bath
is correctly controlled. Brightness depends on the complete
process---not simply adding more brightener.

Over-addition can create its own defects. Follow the commercial additive
supplier's dosing and analytical method.

## Satin nickel

Satin nickel intentionally produces a softer, lower-gloss appearance.
Satin appearance may depend on specialized chemistry and tight
agitation/particle control. Do not treat a dull bright-nickel bath as a
satin process.

## Plating time

Time is selected from:

-   Required thickness
-   Actual deposition rate
-   Current density
-   Cathode efficiency
-   Part geometry
-   Specification requirements

## Thickness control

For production, verify thickness using a suitable measurement method
rather than estimating from color or time alone.

Build a shop-specific deposition-rate curve:

1.  Use representative coupons.
2.  Record surface area and current density.
3.  Plate at controlled temperature and pH.
4.  Measure thickness.
5.  Repeat at several times/current densities within the approved
    window.
6.  Use the resulting data for scheduling.

## Appearance versus thickness

A part can look bright before it has adequate functional thickness.
Appearance is not a thickness measurement.
