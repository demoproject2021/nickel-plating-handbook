# 5. Plating Steel, Copper, Brass, and Stainless Steel

## Steel

Typical logic:

`Mechanical finish → Degrease/clean → Rinse → Activate → Rinse/transfer → Nickel plate`

Prevent flash rust between cleaning/activation and plating. If rust
appears, the surface is no longer properly prepared.

## Copper

Copper is generally straightforward when clean and oxide-free. Avoid
excessive activation that attacks the surface. A uniform
water-break-free rinse and prompt transfer are important.

## Brass

Brass can plate well but contains copper and zinc, so aggressive
chemical treatment can selectively attack the surface. Watch for smut,
discoloration, or etching after activation. Use a brass-compatible
preparation sequence.

## Stainless steel

Stainless steel is intentionally passive and therefore more difficult. A
validated activation plus nickel-strike sequence is commonly used before
the main nickel bath.

## Substrate checklist

Before plating, identify:

-   Exact substrate/alloy when possible
-   Existing coatings
-   Heat treatment or scale
-   Polishing compounds
-   Rust/oxide
-   Porosity
-   Soldered or brazed joints
-   Blind holes that can trap cleaner or gas

Unknown base metals are a major source of inconsistent results.
