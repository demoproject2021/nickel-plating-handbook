# 3. Surface Preparation

Surface preparation is the highest-leverage step in nickel plating. A
perfect bath cannot plate through oil, oxide, polishing compound,
fingerprints, scale, or a passive surface.

## Mechanical preparation

Finish the substrate to the quality expected beneath the nickel. Nickel
generally reproduces underlying scratches, pits, waviness, and polishing
defects rather than hiding them.

Typical operations include sanding, deburring, polishing, blasting, or
other substrate-appropriate finishing.

## Degreasing

Remove bulk oils before precision cleaning. Do not move heavily
contaminated parts directly into a process cleaner because that rapidly
loads the cleaner with oil.

After cleaning, handle parts with clean gloves or dedicated fixtures.

## Water-break test

After cleaning and rinsing, observe the water film.

**Pass:** water sheets uniformly across the surface.

**Fail:** water pulls away, beads, or forms islands.

A failed water-break test means the surface is not ready for plating.
Re-clean it. Do not expect activation or nickel strike to compensate for
grease.

## Rinsing

Use clean rinses and avoid dragging cleaner into activation or plating
tanks. Counterflow rinsing and multiple rinse stages can reduce
contamination in production.

## Common preparation failures

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Symptom                             Likely preparation issue
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Peeling in sheets                   Oil, passive surface, oxide,
                                      delayed transfer

  Bare spots                          Soil, masking contamination,
                                      trapped gas

  Blisters                            Poor cleaning, trapped
                                      contamination, substrate defects

  Rough deposit                       Particles left on part or
                                      introduced during preparation

  Edge-only deposit                   Often electrical/current
                                      distribution, not cleaning alone
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

## Process discipline

Never touch a cleaned plating surface with bare fingers.
