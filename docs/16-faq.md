# 16. Frequently Asked Questions

## What amperage should I use for nickel plating?

Calculate the exposed surface area and multiply it by the current
density specified for your commercial solution. A broad conventional
range may be around 10--50 A/ft², but use the product specification.

## What voltage should I use?

Nickel plating is normally controlled primarily by current/current
density. Use enough voltage for the rectifier to maintain the required
current without exceeding equipment limits.

## What pH should nickel plating solution be?

Many nickel baths are mildly acidic, but there is no universal pH. Use
the manufacturer's specified range.

## What temperature should nickel plating solution be?

Many conventional systems operate warm, often around 120--150°F. The
exact target is product-specific.

## Why is my nickel peeling?

Most adhesion failures begin with cleaning, oxide/passivation,
activation, substrate incompatibility, or transfer delay. Verify
preparation before changing the bath.

## Why are the edges black or burned?

Local current density is probably too high, or mass transfer is
inadequate. Check total amperage, edge geometry, anode spacing,
agitation, temperature, and pH.

## Why do I have pits?

Common causes include gas bubbles, oil, particles, inadequate agitation,
or a wetting-control problem.

## Why is the deposit rough?

Look for suspended solids, anode debris, tank scale, dirty racks, or
contamination introduced during preparation.

## Why is the nickel dull?

Check substrate polish, current density, temperature, pH, electrical
contact, and the condition of the commercial additive system.

## Do I need nickel strike?

It is commonly appropriate for stainless steel and other
passive/difficult surfaces when the validated process calls for it. It
is not a substitute for cleaning.

## Can I plate stainless steel directly?

Direct adhesion is unreliable because stainless steel rapidly
passivates. A suitable activation and nickel-strike sequence is commonly
used.

## How long should I plate?

Time depends on target thickness, actual deposition rate, current
density, efficiency, and geometry. Validate the rate on representative
parts.

## Should I add brightener when plating becomes dull?

Not automatically. Verify operating conditions and run the supplier's
recommended test/analysis first. Blind additions can make the bath
worse.
